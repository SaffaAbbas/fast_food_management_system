RESTAURANT / FAST FOOD MANAGEMENT SOFTWAREComplete Software Development Requirements & Functional Specification

Developer Reference Document | Version 2.0 | October 2026

Purpose: This document defines the functional, workflow, data, calculation, permission, reporting, and operational requirements for a complete Restaurant / Fast Food POS and Management System. It is intended to be used by UI/UX designers, frontend developers, backend developers, database developers, QA testers, and project managers.

1. System Overview

The application shall be a modular, role-based restaurant management platform supporting dine-in, takeaway, delivery, kitchen operations, inventory, recipes, purchases, vendors, expenses, staff, accounting, customers, reservations, promotions, reporting, and multi-branch operations.

1.1 Core Operating Principle

Every sale must create a traceable order and payment record.

Confirmed sales must update relevant inventory according to recipe/stock rules.

Kitchen orders must be visible to authorized kitchen users in real time.

Bills and receipts must be printable and re-printable according to permissions.

Returns/refunds must reverse financial and inventory effects according to configured rules.

All sensitive actions must be logged with user, timestamp, branch, and action.

The system must support configurable tax, discount, service charge, delivery charge, currency, and receipt settings.

The system should be usable on desktop POS screens and responsive on tablets where required.

27. Business Rules & Validation

Do not allow negative stock unless explicitly enabled by administrator.

Do not allow sale of inactive/out-of-stock products unless override permission exists.

Do not allow payment completion with invalid or missing payment data.

Discount above configured limit requires manager permission.

Refund above configured limit requires manager/admin permission.

Stock adjustment requires reason.

Purchase return must reference purchase/received stock where possible.

Deleted transactional records should generally be archived/voided rather than physically deleted.

Price changes must preserve historical transaction prices.

Historical invoices must not change when product master price changes.

All branch-sensitive records must carry branch_id.

All important monetary operations must use decimal-safe financial calculations, not floating-point arithmetic.

35. Developer Implementation Notes

The developer should not treat this document as a flat list of screens. The system must be implemented around business entities and controlled workflows. For example, an order is not simply a printed bill: it connects the customer, table/order type, items, modifiers, kitchen status, payment, inventory consumption, cashier session, and audit trail. Similar relational consistency must be maintained for purchases, returns, stock and vendor balances.

Before coding, the development team should convert this specification into: (1) database ERD, (2) role/permission matrix, (3) screen-by-screen UI wireframes, (4) API contract, (5) state-transition diagrams, (6) validation rules, and (7) QA test cases. Any business rule not explicitly decided by the owner should be made configurable rather than hard-coded where practical.

37. Version 2.0 — QR Table Ordering & Self-Service Kiosk

This section is an extension of the original specification and is part of the development scope. QR Table Ordering and Self-Service Kiosk are not separate systems; both must use the existing central menu, order, kitchen, payment, inventory, customer, reporting and audit architecture.

37.1 QR Table Ordering

Each active restaurant table can have a unique QR code linked to the correct branch, floor and table.

The QR code must open a mobile-first web ordering page without requiring a customer app.

Customer can browse categories/products, search, view product images, choose variations, add-ons and quantities, and enter item/order notes.

Customer can place an order as a guest. Optional name/phone/customer login can be enabled by branch.

Cart must calculate current product prices, discounts, tax, service charge and any configured charges.

QR orders must carry order_source = QR and must store branch_id, table_id and QR session/reference.

QR orders must appear in POS and KDS in real time and follow the same order-status workflow as normal dine-in orders.

Admin can generate, preview, download, print, regenerate and deactivate individual or bulk table QR codes.

QR codes must use secure non-guessable references and must not expose internal database IDs.

Revoked, inactive or invalid QR codes must show a controlled error page rather than opening an order screen.

System must support configurable payment timing: pay before kitchen confirmation, pay at table/counter, or another configured workflow.

Multiple customers may order from the same table. The developer must implement the selected business rule: either separate linked orders or controlled table-session order merging.

Duplicate QR submissions and browser/network retries must be protected through idempotency keys/session tokens.

37.2 Self-Service Kiosk

Kiosk is a touch-optimized customer ordering interface connected to the same central order engine.

Support configurable order types such as Dine-In and Takeaway.

Home screen: restaurant branding, categories, search, featured items, cart and clear navigation.

Product screen: image, description, price, variations, modifiers/add-ons, quantity and notes.

Cart screen: items, quantity controls, discount, tax, service charge and final total.

Payment options must be configurable. Supported modes may include pay-at-counter, card/terminal, bank/online payment or integrated payment gateway.

After confirmation, generate a unique order/token number and send the order to POS and KDS in real time.

Display confirmation and, where configured, estimated preparation time and order status.

Automatically clear the session and return to the home screen after configurable inactivity.

Kiosk must run in locked full-screen kiosk mode and prevent access to the operating system or unrelated applications.

Admin/operator can create a kiosk record, assign it to a branch/location, activate/deactivate it, put it in maintenance mode and view its health/online status.

Kiosk requests must be idempotent so network retries cannot create duplicate orders or payments.

If the backend is unavailable, the kiosk must clearly show offline/unavailable status and must never falsely show an order as confirmed.

37.3 Unified Self-Ordering Workflow

Customer scans a table QR or starts a kiosk session.

System identifies the branch and, for QR, the linked table.

System loads the active menu and availability.

Customer selects products, variations, modifiers and notes.

System calculates price, discounts, tax and charges.

Customer reviews and confirms the cart.

Payment is completed if the configured workflow requires pre-payment.

Central order service creates the order and returns an order/token number.

Order source is recorded as QR or KIOSK.

Relevant items are routed to KDS/kitchen stations.

Customer sees confirmation and order status.

Kitchen prepares and updates status.

POS/staff can monitor or manage the order according to permissions.

Order is served/collected and finalized.

Payment, inventory, customer, reporting and audit records are posted using the same rules as POS orders.

37.4 QR & Kiosk Administration

QR Management: table mapping, QR generation, preview, print, download, regeneration, deactivation and bulk generation.

Kiosk Management: kiosk ID, branch, location, device name, status, maintenance mode, active menu, supported order types and payment configuration.

Self-Ordering Settings: enable/disable QR, enable/disable kiosk, customer identification, staff approval, payment timing, ordering hours, timeout and unavailable-item behavior.

Order Source values should be extensible: POS, QR, KIOSK, ONLINE, APP, DELIVERY_PLATFORM and future channels.

Analytics should compare QR, kiosk and POS performance: order count, revenue, average order value, top products and peak ordering periods.

Only authorized users can regenerate QR codes, disable kiosks, change self-ordering configuration or override self-service orders.

37.8 UI/UX Requirements

Use large touch targets, persistent cart access, clear price visibility and minimal checkout steps.

Kiosk should use large buttons, high-contrast text, clear back/home controls and persistent cart summary.

Kiosk must automatically reset to a clean home state after inactivity.

37.9 Business Rules & Validation

Every QR order must reference a valid active QR/table/branch context.

Every kiosk order must reference a valid active kiosk and branch.

Price and availability must be validated again at final order submission.

QR/Kiosk orders must use the same tax, promotion, discount, recipe and inventory rules as POS orders.

Staff edits, cancellations, refunds and overrides must follow existing permission rules and audit logging.

If payment is mandatory before confirmation, failed/expired payment must not create a completed paid order.

QR regeneration/revocation must not modify historical orders.

Kiosk session data must be cleared on timeout/reset.

37.10 QA & Acceptance Tests

Valid QR opens the correct restaurant/table.

Invalid, expired and revoked QR codes are rejected.

Two customers can order from the same table according to the selected table-session rule.

Duplicate taps, browser retries and network retries do not create duplicate orders.

QR order reaches POS and the correct KDS station.

QR payment success/failure/timeout behaves correctly.

Kiosk supports every enabled order type.

Kiosk session resets after inactivity.

Kiosk offline state does not falsely confirm orders.

Kiosk activation/deactivation and branch assignment work correctly.

QR/Kiosk discounts, promotions, taxes, modifiers and inventory match POS calculations.

QR/Kiosk orders appear correctly in sales, product, profit, customer and payment reports.

Permissions prevent unauthorized QR/kiosk configuration.

Audit logs capture configuration changes, order overrides, cancellations and refunds.

38. Recommended Future Product Roadmap

The following features are recommended for future versions after the core POS + QR + Kiosk system is stable. They should be designed as extensions of the same central architecture rather than separate applications.

Customer Mobile App — ordering, loyalty, order history, saved addresses, offers and push notifications.

Online Ordering Website — branded menu, delivery/takeaway ordering, customer accounts, coupons and online payments.

Omnichannel Order Inbox — one screen for POS, QR, kiosk, website, mobile app and third-party delivery orders.

Digital Payments — gateways, card terminals, wallets and automatic payment reconciliation.

Advanced Loyalty/CRM — customer tiers, vouchers, referrals, targeted offers and customer segmentation.

Marketing Automation — WhatsApp/SMS/email campaigns triggered by purchases, inactivity, loyalty and promotions.

Smart Inventory — predicted reorder quantities, supplier lead-time analysis, expiry alerts and wastage insights.

AI Forecasting — demand prediction, sales forecasting, staffing suggestions and inventory planning.

AI Business Assistant — natural-language questions such as today's sales, best-selling item, low-stock risk and branch comparison.

Kitchen Optimization — station workload balancing, preparation-time analytics and intelligent ticket prioritization.

Franchise Mode — head-office controls, branch onboarding, standardized menus, permissions and franchise reporting.

Delivery Platform Integrations — synchronize orders, menus, status and settlement data with supported delivery providers.

Accounting Integration — automatic posting of sales, purchases, expenses, receivables/payables and settlements.

Multilingual & Accessibility — Urdu/English and other configurable languages, larger text and accessibility options.

Customer Feedback — digital receipts with rating/feedback links and issue tracking.

Advanced Security — MFA, device management, rate limiting, encrypted secrets, backup verification and disaster recovery.

Offline-First Expansion — local POS/KDS operation with queued synchronization and explicit conflict resolution.

API/Integration Marketplace — documented APIs/webhooks for CRM, accounting, payments, messaging and external services.

39. Development Priority & Start-Work Instructions

Priority 1: Build the central order engine and ensure POS, QR and Kiosk all create the same canonical order structure.

Priority 2: Implement QR table mapping, secure public QR tokens and customer mobile ordering.

Priority 3: Implement Kiosk device/session management and touch-first customer UI.

Priority 4: Connect both channels to KDS, POS, payments, inventory, customer records, notifications and reports.

Priority 5: Complete permissions, audit logs, idempotency, error recovery and offline/network handling.

Priority 6: Complete QA using the Version 2.0 acceptance checklist before production rollout.

Before coding, developer must produce: ERD updates, role/permission matrix updates, screen-by-screen wireframes for QR and kiosk, API contracts, state-transition diagrams, payment flow, error/offline flows and QA test cases.

Business decisions that are not fixed by the owner must be configurable rather than hard-coded.
